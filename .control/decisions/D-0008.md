## Contexto

The product must support 2D motion graphics and 3D scenes. Treating 3D
as a separate mode or application creates two timelines, two evaluation
paths and two mental models; compositing 3D over 2D becomes a special
case.

## Decision

Use one composition model and one explicit layer ordering for 2D and
3D. A 3D layer is a compositable item, like a 2D layer: it renders its
own scene (camera, lights, meshes) to a layer buffer, and the compositor
places it in the same ordered stack. Both kinds share the timeline,
transforms, effects, masks and precomps.

## Consecuencias

Gains:
- One mental model and one timeline for the user.
- Arbitrary interleaving of 2D and 3D layers, adjustments and precomps.
- One evaluation and caching model.

Costs:
- The renderer must manage depth and alpha buffers per 3D layer.
- Layer ordering must be explicit and unambiguous in the document.
- Some 3D-specific controls need a clean home in the shared inspector.

Pending:
- Define ordering semantics for nested precomps and adjustment layers.
- Define depth interaction between 3D layers and 2D layers that receive
  depth, if any.
- Decide whether 3D cameras can be shared across layers or are owned by
  the 3D layer.
